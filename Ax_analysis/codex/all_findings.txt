====================================================================================================
WINDOWS & GHOSTS — HOW DO YOU PICK THE RIGHT WINDOW?
====================================================================================================
[1] (high) The window-server list is overwhelmingly ghosts: 130 of 161 windows (80.7%) across 24 apps are phantom entries, and every ghost shares the triple signature visible=false AND is_focused=false AND title==app_name, combined with one of a small fixed set of geometries.
    EV: 161 windows total across 24 app dirs with both windows.json and full.json. Ghost geometry clusters: 2560x30 at (0,0) x56, 1280x29 at (2560,536) x56, 500x500 at (0,940) x7, 0x0 x5, 1x1 at (0,0) x4, 64x64 at (0,940) x2. All 130 have visible=false, focused=false, title==app_name. Zero of the 130 flagged ghosts matched the app's AX root bounds within 5px (false-ghost count = 0).
    EX: None found in 161 windows. Note real windows CAN have title==app_name and visible=false (Calendar 935x598, Music 980x580, Reminders 768x692, UTM 1393x1410) — the geometry clause is what makes the predicate safe; title/visible alone would misfire on 8+ real windows.
    RULE: ghost(w) := (not w.visible) and (not w.is_focused) and (w.title == w.app_name) and ( (w.h <= 30 and w.w >= 1000) or (w.w, w.h) in {(0,0),(1,1),(64,64),(500,500)} ). Measured on this corpus: flags 130/161 windows, 0 false positives against AX-root ground truth.

[2] (high) Menu-bar-strip ghosts come in an exact, invariant quantum: every app that has them has exactly 4 copies of the 2560x30@(0,0) strip and exactly 4 copies of the 1280x29@(2560,536) strip — one pair of strips per display, times 4.
    EV: 14 of 24 apps (antigravity_ide, brave_browser, claude, discord, finder, gemini, google_chrome, keynote, microsoft_word, notion, safari, telegram, vlc, yt_music) each show precisely (4,4); the other 10 apps show (0,0). 56+56 = 112 of the 130 ghosts (86%) are these strips. Strip positions encode the display layout: display 1 menu bar at (0,0) 2560 wide, display 2 menu bar at (2560,536) 1280 wide.
    EX: TextEdit has a 678x64 invisible window (a sheet) — width 678 < 1000 so the rule correctly leaves it alone; no real window in the corpus is a >=1000-wide, <=30-tall strip.
    RULE: if w.height <= 30 and w.width >= 1000 and w.y in {0, <display2_origin_y>} then drop as menu-bar ghost. Cheap standalone form: drop any window with height <= 30 and width >= 1000.

[3] (high) is_focused is a perfect but rarely-available signal: when any window carries is_focused=true, that single window's bounds equal the AX root bounds exactly, but only foreground-captured apps have one.
    EV: 6 of 24 apps have a focused window (claude, discord, keynote, microsoft_word, notion, telegram); in 6/6 cases its bounds match full.json root bounds exactly (0px tolerance). Never more than one focused window per app; a ghost is never focused. 18/24 apps (75%) have no focused window at all.
    EX: None. But absence of is_focused is the common case, so this is a fast path, not an algorithm.
    RULE: if any(w.is_focused): return that w — trust its bounds verbatim (100% accuracy, 6/6). Never fall through to other heuristics when is_focused exists.

[4] (high) visible=true is a trap: for apps whose real window is minimized/on another Space, the ONLY visible window is a tiny Dock-thumbnail proxy (both dims <= 200px) whose bounds are wrong by 34x-126x in area but whose title is the real window's exact title.
    EV: 16 visible windows in corpus: 8 are real full windows matching AX root, 6 are thumbnails (antigravity 178x161 vs AX 2560x1410 = 126x area lie; firefox 92x108 vs 1249x923 = 116x; safari 108x151 vs 1087x803; brave 129x146 vs 1036x802; vlc 125x157 vs 1280x803; yt_music 125x145 vs 1280x803), 1 is a secondary OBS dialog (600x428). Title check: 6/6 thumbnails carry the real window's title ('New Tab', 'Start Page', 'YC — CLI tool integr...', song title, etc.), never the app name. Naive largest-visible heuristic scores only 8/14 where visible windows exist.
    EX: No real target window in the corpus is <= 200px in both dims, but small utility palettes could be in principle — pair the size test with a follow-up AX-bounds confirmation.
    RULE: thumbnail(w) := w.width <= 200 and w.height <= 200 and not ghost(w). Never use a thumbnail's bounds for targeting; use its title to identify the real window, then take bounds from the AX tree root. Two more thumbnails are invisible (gemini 137x187, whatsapp 130x96 at x=-146), so key the rule on dims, not on visible.

[5] (high) The Antigravity 178x161-vs-2560x1410 bounds lie is a class, not an incident: in 9 of 24 apps (37.5%) NO window-server entry matches the AX root bounds at all — windows.json alone cannot yield correct target bounds for these apps.
    EV: Unwinnable apps: antigravity_ide, brave_browser, finder, firefox, gemini, safari, vlc, whatsapp, yt_music. In 8 of the 9, a <= 200px thumbnail stands in for the real window; in Finder, all 9 WS entries are ghosts while the AX tree shows a real 920x436 'Recents' window at (2753,657) — the real window is entirely absent from the window list. Conversely, in all 15 winnable apps the match is EXACT (tol=0 and tol=5 both give 15/24 — the 5px slack buys nothing).
    EX: None: window-server bounds are either exactly right or wildly wrong (min area error among mismatches is 34x); there is no 'slightly off' middle ground in this corpus.
    RULE: After picking a candidate from windows.json, verify: candidate.bounds == ax_root.bounds (exact equality). If no non-ghost candidate passes, the window is minimized/other-Space — take bounds from the AX tree root and treat the windows.json entry (if any) only as an identity/title witness.

[6] (high) Best-window algorithm — ghost-filter, then focused, then largest visible non-thumbnail, then largest non-thumbnail — picks a window whose bounds exactly equal the AX root in 15/15 apps where that is achievable, i.e. 100% on winnable apps and 62.5% (15/24) overall, with the remaining 9 detectable as the thumbnail/absent class.
    EV: Measured per app: correct on calendar, claude, discord, google_chrome (beats 3 extra non-strip ghosts incl. two invisible 1033x89/88 phantom bars), keynote, microsoft_word, music, notes, notion, obs (picks 1086x757 main over 600x428 dialog), reminders, telegram, textedit (picks 893x448 over 678x64 sheet), utm, zoom_us (picks 1280x960 over two stale 2nd-display entries). All 9 failures are exactly the unwinnable apps.
    EX: OBS: two visible non-thumb windows (main 1086x757 + dialog 600x428); largest-by-area picks the main window correctly here, but a large modal covering the main window would invert this — the AX-root equality check is the safety net.
    RULE: candidates = [w for w in windows if not ghost(w)]; if any focused: return it; nonthumb = [w in candidates if w.w > 200 or w.h > 200]; pick = max(visible nonthumb, key=area) or max(nonthumb, key=area); if pick and pick.bounds == ax_root.bounds: use pick; else: use ax_root bounds directly (thumbnail-class app). If candidates is empty (Finder case): go straight to the AX tree.

[7] (high) title==app_name is a strong ghost correlate but not a ghost proof: 100% of ghosts have it, yet 10 real windows also have it, so it works only as a tiebreaker/necessary condition, never as a sufficient one.
    EV: 130/130 ghosts have title==app_name. Real windows with title==app_name: claude 600x600 (app literally titles its window 'Claude'), microsoft_word 1087x803 ('Open new and recent files' capture had WS title==app), telegram 1087x803, calendar, music, reminders, utm, whatsapp, gemini, safari 1280x803. Inverting: a window whose title differs from app_name is never a ghost (0/31 non-ghosts... all 8 thumbnails and most real windows have distinct titles).
    EX: Apps whose real window title legitimately equals the app name (Claude, Telegram at idle, VLC's 'VLC media player' thumbnail) — 10 cases in this corpus.
    RULE: if w.title != w.app_name: w is definitely not a ghost (0 counterexamples in 161). if w.title == w.app_name: undetermined — apply the geometry predicate.

[8] (medium) Multi-display coordinates are benign in this corpus: display 2 occupies x in [2560, 3840] (origin (2560,536), 1280 wide), a window with x >= 2560 is simply on display 2, and the only negative-x window is an off-screen minimized WhatsApp proxy at x=-146.
    EV: x range across 161 windows: -146 to 2995. Display-2 real windows: vlc AX 1280x803@(2560,565), safari AX 1087x803@(2753,565), obs 1086x757@(2753,609), finder AX 920x436@(2753,657), gemini AX 1087x803@(2753,565), brave AX 1036x802@(2633,566) — all fit within x+w <= 3840. Exactly 1 negative-x window (whatsapp 130x96@(-146,627), invisible, dims<=200 = thumbnail class). No superwide (w>2560 spanning) windows except antigravity's 2560x1410 AX root at x=18, which stays on display 1's logical span.
    EX: The x=-146 WhatsApp entry is off-screen AND a thumbnail; off-screen alone did not occur for any bounds-trustworthy window, so treat off-screen as corroborating evidence of the minimized class, not an independent filter.
    RULE: Do NOT reject windows for x >= screen_width or x < 0 per se; derive display rects from the menu-bar-strip ghosts (each distinct strip position (x,y,w) = one display's top edge) and only flag a window as off-screen if its rect intersects no display rect.

[9] (high) The 500x500@(0,940), 64x64@(0,940), 1x1@(0,0) and 0x0 entries are Electron/Chromium helper-surface ghosts pinned to fixed screen anchors: 500x500 and 64x64 are bottom-left-anchored (y+h = 1440 = display-1 bottom edge exactly), and they appear only for Chromium-family apps.
    EV: 500x500@(0,940) x7: brave, gemini, google_chrome, safari(!), yt_music, notes, zoom_us. 64x64@(0,940) x2: antigravity_ide, finder. 1x1@(0,0) x4: brave x2, google_chrome x2. 0x0 x5: calendar x3 @(0,1440), firefox @(2129,695), obs @(0,0). All 18 are visible=false, title==app_name. 940+500=1440 and both positions sit at display-1 edges.
    EX: The Chromium-family attribution is looser (safari and notes also show 500x500), so key the rule purely on dimensions, not on framework.
    RULE: drop w if (w.w,w.h) in {(0,0),(1,1),(64,64),(500,500)} — degenerate or icon-sized surfaces are never target windows (0 counterexamples in 161; smallest real/proxy window is 92x108).

[10] (high) An empty or near-empty window list does not mean the app has no window: apps can present 1-2 entries that are all ghosts or thumbnails while a full-size, fully-hydrated window exists in the AX tree, so 'no candidate survived the ghost filter' must route to the AX tree, never to 'no window' /failure.
    EV: Finder: 9/9 windows are ghosts, yet AX tree has a real 920x436 window. Firefox: 2 entries (one 0x0 ghost, one 92x108 thumbnail), AX tree has 1249x923@(1311,273). WhatsApp: single 130x96 invisible off-screen entry, AX tree has 1087x803@(383,153). In all three the AX capture succeeded with a full tree.
    EX: Truly windowless apps would also hit this branch; disambiguate by whether the AX root returns bounds (all 24 apps here did, including opaque Telegram whose 1-node root still reported real bounds 1087x803 matching its WS focused window exactly).
    RULE: if [w for w in windows if not ghost(w) and not thumbnail(w)] == []: do not report 'window not found'; snapshot the AX tree and use ax_root.bounds as the window rect (worked in 3/3 such apps in this corpus).

ROLE & ACTION VOCABULARY — PER-FRAMEWORK ACTION CAPABILITY MATRIX AND ROLE NORMALIZATION
====================================================================================================
[1] (high) The tool emits a closed vocabulary of 49 roles across all 24 captured apps, and it contains NO 'tab', 'menuitem', or 'listitem' role — those UI concepts always surface under proxy roles.
    EV: Union of roles over every full.json+forced.json (music/forced.json excluded as contaminated — its data.app is 'YT Music', size 708944 == yt_music/forced.json): 49 distinct roles (alert…window); 'tab' in vocab = False, 'menuitem' = False, 'listitem' = False. Tabs appear as tablist>radiobutton in brave_browser (2), google_chrome (1), discord (4), notion (4), firefox (2), and microsoft_word's ribbon (10); as tablist>button in safari (2, under a scrollarea); as tablist>group in yt_music/notion/firefox. List items appear as list>group (finder 1516, keynote 310), outline>treeitem (gemini 118, finder 54, textedit 60), table>row (word 26, notes 4), list>row (obs 5).
    RULE: Normalize against the closed set: if node.role not in the 49-role vocabulary, treat as 'unknown'. Concept 'tab' := any descendant of a role=='tablist' node (depth<=2) whose role is in {radiobutton, button, group} and whose available_actions contains Click or Toggle. Concept 'list item' := direct child of {list, outline, table, grid} with role in {group, treeitem, row, cell, button}.

[2] (high) Toggle is an exact biconditional with role in {checkbox, radiobutton, switch}: every such node exposes Toggle and no other role ever does, in every framework.
    EV: 175 Toggle actions total across all captures; per (framework, role) coverage is 100% in all 13 observed pairs: appkit checkbox 18/18, radiobutton 12/12, switch 12/12; electron radiobutton 18/18, switch 2/2; chromium-browser radiobutton 3/3, checkbox 1/1; gecko radiobutton 4/4, switch 10/10; webkit-native switch 50/50, radiobutton 26/26; custom-cpp (Word/Zoom) radiobutton 16/16; qt-cef (OBS) checkbox 3/3. Sum of role-node counts = 175 = total Toggle count, so no Toggle appears on any other role.
    RULE: if role in {checkbox, radiobutton, switch} then assume Toggle is available (and use it as the activation verb); if 'Toggle' in available_actions then classify the node as a toggleable control regardless of role name.

[3] (high) Expand and Collapse always co-occur, and Expand on role==combobox is universal across frameworks, while menubutton support is framework-dependent (missing in Catalyst/Swift and Word).
    EV: Expand count == Collapse count == 83 with 0 nodes having one without the other (checked all captures). Combobox coverage 100% everywhere: electron 34/34 (notion, claude), chromium-browser 22/22 (brave, chrome), gecko 4/4 (firefox), webkit-native 2/2, appkit 4/4, custom-cpp 1/1. Menubutton: appkit 10/12, qt-cef 2/2, webkit-native 2/2, but catalyst-or-swift (gemini/whatsapp) 0/4 and custom-cpp (word) 0/2. Word disclosure 2/2.
    EX: 2 AppKit menubuttons lack Expand; Catalyst (gemini, whatsapp) and Word menubuttons never expose it.
    RULE: if role=='combobox' then Expand is usable to open it in any framework. if role=='menubutton' and 'Expand' not in available_actions then fall back to Click (do not treat missing Expand as non-interactive). Never emit Collapse without first checking 'Expand' in actions — they are one capability.

[4] (high) Select is a container-level action: it appears only on {list, outline, table, combobox}, never on the items themselves (treeitem/cell/row never carry Select) in any framework.
    EV: All 89 Select actions sit on: appkit outline 17/list 8/table 2 (finder, notes, vlc), electron list 4/outline 5 (discord, notion), chromium-browser combobox 19/list 1, gecko combobox 2/list 4, webkit-native list 12/combobox 2 (safari, yt_music), custom-cpp list 4/table 2 (word, zoom), custom-iwork outline 1/list 2 (keynote), qt-cef list 2 (obs), catalyst outline 2 (gemini). Zero Select on the 385 treeitem, 454 cell, or 31 row nodes.
    RULE: To select an item, do NOT look for Select on the item node; either Click the item, or invoke Select on the nearest ancestor with role in {list, outline, table, combobox} and 'Select' in its available_actions.

[5] (high) Click presence is a trustworthy interactivity signal in every framework EXCEPT Gecko, where Firefox attaches Click to virtually every node including static text and toolbars.
    EV: Click on non-interactive roles (statictext/group/image): gecko 202/208 (97.1%) — firefox forced.json has Click on statictext 37/37, toolbar 3/3, splitter 3/3, tablist 2/2; versus electron 0/1252 (0.0%), chromium-browser 3/205 (1.5%), appkit 28/1654 (1.7%), custom-cpp 1/45 (2.2%), webkit-native 42/996 (4.2%), custom-iwork 0/313. Meanwhile Click coverage on role==button is 100% in 19/23 apps (e.g. calendar 198/198, gemini 150/150, notion 97/97, zoom 60/60).
    EX: qt-cef small-sample: OBS shows Click on 5/6 groups, so Qt may also be over-permissive.
    RULE: if framework=='gecko': ignore 'Click' in available_actions as an interactivity filter and rely on role in {button, link, checkbox, radiobutton, switch, combobox, textfield, treeitem, menubutton}. Otherwise: 'Click' in available_actions ⇒ node is actionable.

[6] (high) RightClick is ambient noise in all web-rendered trees (Electron, Gecko, WebKit, Chromium) — it appears on the vast majority of static/decorative nodes and must never be read as a context-menu affordance signal; in native AppKit/iWork it is rare and meaningful.
    EV: RightClick on statictext/group/image: electron 1202/1252 (96.0%), gecko 208/208 (100%), webkit-native 968/996 (97.2%), chromium-browser 151/205 (73.7%), catalyst 32/54 (59.3%) vs appkit 28/1654 (1.7%), custom-iwork 0/313 (0%), qt-cef 0/6, custom-cpp 9/45 (20%). Globally RightClick (5942) outnumbers Click (4606). Safari's 148 disabled clickless buttons still carry RightClick.
    RULE: Never use 'RightClick' in available_actions to rank or filter candidate targets. Interactivity score should count only {Click, Toggle, Expand, Select, SetValue on input roles, TypeText}; treat RightClick and SetFocus as ambient.

[7] (high) Disabled controls keep their Click action in every framework except WebKit-native and Qt, which strip actions instead — so 'is this button actionable' needs a framework-conditional check.
    EV: Click retained on buttons with state 'disabled': appkit 27/27, chromium-browser 6/6, electron 4/4, gecko 4/4, custom-cpp 8/8, catalyst 2/2, custom-iwork 1/1 — but webkit-native 12/188 (6.4%) and qt-cef 0/8 (0%). This explains the button-Click coverage outliers: safari 32/242 (13.2% — its 210 clickless buttons carry states disabled 172 / offscreen 153, action sets {RightClick}:148, {}:56, {SetFocus}:6), obs 28/36 (77.8%), reminders 26/46.
    RULE: actionable(button) := ('disabled' not in states) AND (framework in {webkit-native, qt-cef} ? 'Click' in available_actions : True). Conversely, in WebKit/Qt a role=='button' without Click should be skipped, not clicked via coordinates.

[8] (high) In AppKit and iWork, role=='textfield' is overwhelmingly a LABEL, not an input — SetValue is present on ~all of them and means nothing; the presence of SetFocus in available_actions is the editability discriminator.
    EV: AppKit textfields: 1596 nodes, 37.4% of all AppKit nodes; 1591/1596 (99.7%) expose SetValue, including Finder's 1516 filename labels (list>group>textfield) and Keynote's 310 slide-navigator labels — none of which are editable. SetFocus coverage separates them: finder 0/1516, keynote 0/310 vs calendar 14/14, vlc 2/2, notes 5/6, reminders 14/16, textedit 2/42. TypeText on AppKit textfields: 2/1596 (both in notes, and both also carry SetFocus). Web frameworks have no such inflation: electron 35 textfields, chromium 6 — all genuine inputs.
    EX: textedit shows 2/42 with SetFocus but its main document area is a separate scrollarea/textarea; UTM anomaly: 4 role=='button' nodes carry SetValue (Click+RightClick+SetFocus+SetValue, no name) — likely toolbar toggle buttons.
    RULE: Role normalization: if framework in {appkit, custom-iwork} and role=='textfield' and 'SetFocus' not in available_actions then treat as statictext (read-only label; never SetValue it). If 'SetFocus' in actions (or 'TypeText' present) treat as text input.

[9] (high) TypeText exists only in web-rendered frameworks: 100% of Electron and Chromium textfields expose it, native frameworks essentially never do — so typing strategy must branch by framework.
    EV: TypeText total 68. Coverage on textfield: electron 35/35 (notion 34, discord 1), chromium-browser 6/6 plus combobox 19/22 (brave omnibox etc.), gecko combobox 2/4, webkit-native combobox 2/2, catalyst 2/6 (gemini) — versus appkit 2/1596 (0.1%), custom-iwork 0/310, custom-cpp 0/4 (word), qt 0.
    RULE: To enter text: if 'TypeText' in available_actions use it. Else if framework in {appkit, custom-cpp, custom-iwork, catalyst-or-swift} and node is a real input (SetFocus present), use SetFocus then synthesized keystrokes, or SetValue for wholesale replacement. Absence of TypeText on a native field does NOT mean it is not typable.

[10] (high) The mass-'focused' state is a native list-view artifact: a focused NSTableView/list propagates 'focused' to every item row, so native captures show hundreds of focused nodes while web-rendered captures show at most ~4 (the real focus chain).
    EV: finder full.json: 779/1571 nodes focused, decomposed as group 751 + cell 27 + list 1 — i.e. one focused list plus all its item groups (matches the known 'Finder 779'). Same shape in keynote forced.json: 337/689 = group 310 + cell 26 + list 1. Also word 40/106 per capture (38.3%), textedit 51/129, gemini 27.2%, vlc 28.1%. Web-rendered: discord 2, firefox 2, safari 2, notion 4, yt_music 3, zoom 2 (0.2–1.8%).
    RULE: if count(nodes with 'focused') > 3 in a capture, treat 'focused' as list-membership noise: the truly focused element is the unique node whose 'focused' ancestors form a chain to the window and whose role is in {list, outline, table, textfield, webarea} — in the propagation pattern, take the focused container (role list/outline), not its focused item children. If count <= 3, the deepest focused node is the keyboard focus.

[11] (high) Offscreen recovery is framework-split: web-rendered frameworks attach ScrollTo directly to offscreen nodes; native frameworks attach nothing to the node and expose Scroll only on the enclosing scrollarea.
    EV: ScrollTo on nodes with state 'offscreen': electron 466/466 (100%), gecko 51/51 (100%), chromium-browser 22/26 (84.6%), webkit-native 987/1185 (83.3%) — versus appkit 0/3181 (finder alone has 3042 offscreen nodes, 96% of its tree), custom-iwork 0/604, custom-cpp 0/46, catalyst 0/228. The Scroll action lives only on containers in native apps: finder scrollarea 4 + browser 2, word scrollarea 4, calendar scrollarea 2, keynote scrollarea 4 + browser 1. Catalyst exception: whatsapp puts Scroll on items themselves (button 22, group 4, link 2, statictext 2).
    EX: WhatsApp (Catalyst) is the one app putting Scroll on leaf items; webkit's 17% ScrollTo-less offscreen nodes are mostly disabled Safari favorites buttons.
    RULE: To reach an offscreen node: if 'ScrollTo' in its available_actions, invoke it. Else walk ancestors to the nearest role=='scrollarea' (or 'browser') with 'Scroll' in available_actions and scroll that container, then re-snapshot. Never coordinate-click a node with 'offscreen' in states.

[12] (high) The 'unknown' role is bimodal and decidable by its action set: unknown+Click is a real custom control (Zoom's main nav tabs), unknown with empty actions is decorative filler to skip (AppKit private subclasses and a Safari banner).
    EV: 27 unknown-role node observations total. Actionable cluster: zoom_us 6 unique nodes per capture, parent role 'group', actions [Click, RightClick], named, uniform 84x50 px — the Home/Chat-style nav rail (plus 1 unknown window strip with RightClick only). Inert cluster: safari 1 (parent window, 1087x38, no actions), calendar 1 (parent group, no actions), notes 1 + reminders 3 (parent 'cell', named, no actions). No unknown node anywhere carries SetValue/Toggle/Expand.
    RULE: if role=='unknown': if 'Click' in available_actions treat as button (clickable; use its name for matching); else drop the node from the interactive index but keep its name as a text label for its parent cell.

[13] (medium) Framework classes have distinct role-histogram signatures usable as a capture fingerprint: AppKit is textfield-heavy (37.4%), web-rendered is group-heavy (40-55%) with statictext for labels, custom-C++ (Word/Zoom) is the only class emitting 'row' at scale, and Catalyst inflates cell/treeitem (44.6% combined).
    EV: Role share of top roles per class (full+forced): electron group 55.2%/statictext 17.0%/textfield 2.1%; chromium-browser group 39.6%/button 18.8%; appkit group 38.0%/textfield 37.4%/statictext only 0.7%; catalyst (gemini+whatsapp) button 37.4%/treeitem 22.3%/cell 22.3%; custom-cpp button 32.8%/cell 24.4%/row 8.1%/unknown 4.7%; custom-iwork group 44.8%/textfield 44.4%; qt-cef button 58.1%/row 8.1%; webkit-native group 37.8%/statictext 19.9%/link 14.5%. 'row' occurs only in custom-cpp (26, word tables), qt-cef (5, obs list) and trivially elsewhere.
    EX: Histogram shares shift with which window was open at capture time; telegram excluded (2-node opaque tree); antigravity forced capture failed (RefMap >1MB) so its electron share is from full.json only.
    RULE: Label-concept normalization per framework for text extraction: label := statictext (electron, chromium, gecko, webkit, custom-cpp) | textfield-without-SetFocus (appkit, custom-iwork) | cell.name or unknown.name (custom-cpp Zoom/Word) | treeitem/cell descendants' concatenated names (catalyst). Item-concept: treeitem|cell (appkit, catalyst), group-child-of-list (electron, finder list view, keynote), row (word, obs).

ELEMENT IDENTITY & SELECTORS — HOW TO ADDRESS AN ELEMENT RELIABLY
====================================================================================================
[1] (high) native_id is a framework-determined bonus, never a dependable primary key: its kind is dictated by rendering stack (ax_identifier for AppKit/Qt/native, ax_dom_identifier for web-rendered), and coverage on interactive elements ranges from 0.1% to 94.3% by app.
    EV: Best capture per app (forced, else full; music/forced.json excluded because it actually captured YT Music — verified via its data.app='YT Music'). Interactive-element native_id coverage: OBS 94.3% (50/53), Safari 88.2%, Chrome 75.0%, Firefox 50.6%, VLC 47.2%, Reminders 32.7%, Calendar 20.7% — but Finder 0.1% (1/798), Keynote 0.3%, Word 0.0%, Discord 2.0%, Claude 0.0%. Rollup by framework class: Qt 75.0% ax_identifier, gecko 50.0% ax_dom, chromium 25.7% ax_dom, electron 16.4% ax_dom, AppKit only 5.0% ax_identifier on 1464 interactives. Zoom and YT Music emit both kinds in one tree (zoom 5 ax_identifier + 5 ax_dom).
    RULE: if node.native_id exists: use it as the first selector key, qualified by kind ('ax_identifier' vs 'ax_dom_identifier' are distinct namespaces — never compare across kinds). else fall through to (role, normalized_name). Never fail a resolution because native_id is absent: expect it on <21% of interactives in AppKit/Electron apps.

[2] (high) ax_identifier values split into three stability classes: semantic hand-authored ids (most stable), '_NS:<n>' Interface-Builder ordinals (stable within an app build only), and ids that embed per-session/user data (session-scoped).
    EV: Value shapes across apps: semantic — OBS 58/58 are full dot-separated widget paths (e.g. OBSApp.OBSBasic.centralwidget...previewZoomOutButton), Safari 123 semantic vs 6 _NS:n, Calendar 36 semantic; _NS:n dominated — VLC 22/22, Reminders 23/35, Telegram root 1/1. Session/user-data-bearing: Safari window id embeds a per-window UUID and an IsSecure flag; Calendar sidebar ids embed the calendar-list title (pattern 'calendarlist-title-field:<list name>'), i.e. the id itself carries user content.
